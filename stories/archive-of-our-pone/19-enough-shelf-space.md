# Enough Shelf Space

As more and more stories about the Bearers and their friends (and associates, and vague acquaintances, and…) came in, an issue soon made itself known. The new town library had been filled, yes. Several times over, even. The question now was what to do with the remaindered, well, *remainders*, and that question has getting increasingly pressing. This was both metaphorically and literally the case; the soil outside of Twilight’s castle had already sunk by half an inch beneath the aggregated works by the time she started making plans.

Thankfully, while her own collection had never needed *advanced* library science, Twilight had spent more than one lazy afternoon figuring out how she’d store and sort thousands upon thousands of books. Now she finally had an opportunity to make that dream a reality, especially once she threw a little royal weight around to get everything she needed.

(Normally, she hated to lean on her authority as a princess, but this was for *books*. This was *important.*)

Within a week, the setup was complete and the collection had been successfully imported. Flush with accomplishment, Twilight sought out the nearest friend who hadn’t helped with the process so she could ~~show it off~~ demonstrate her achievement to an appreciative audience.

She had planned out a detailed lecture about the efforts that went into assembling the extradimensional space, then populating it with books that could, would, or might have been written on top of the ones that already had been. She’d carved out an entire five-minute sidebar praising Pinkie Pie for her invaluable help in designing the parts that weren’t, in the strictest sense, *possible.*

Rainbow Dash interrupted her less than a minute in. “So… it’s a library,” she said, looking around. She tilted her head as she tracked the fractal swirl of one of the closer bookshelves.

In hindsight, maybe Twilight should have delivered the lecture *before* showing Rainbow the end result. Or just skipped it altogether. Or gone with a different friend. “Yes. It’s a library. The ultimate library in terms of stories about us and our friends!”

“That’s cool, I guess,” said Rainbow, shrugging her wings at one of the greatest feats of planar engineering since Discord’s last spring cleaning. “So how many Daring Do books are there?”

Twilight gave her a flat look. “Rainbow Dash, I’m glad the series got you into reading for fun, but there’s more to literature than Daring Do books.”

“For some reason, sure. But how many are we talking here?” Rainbow arched her head up, watching as countless spines curved around them in hyperbolic shelving units that folded into themselves beyond the horizon. She betrayed no appreciation for or awareness of the effort that had gone into sorting it all. “A thousand? A million? A bajillion?”

“The hypothetical titles are still instantiating, so there are only one hundred and fifty thousand titles total at the moment.” Twilight couldn’t help but add, “Also, ‘bajillion,’ isn’t a real number.”

Rainbow snickered and nudged her with a wing. “Yeah, that was just to wind you up.” She took flight, looking around the room. “But come on, how many more stories of the second coolest pegasus ever are there?”

“That *does* bring me to the next part of the lec— the tour.” One of the many things Twilight’s friendship with Rainbow Dash had taught her was how to think on her hooves. She guided her friend to an imposing filing cabinet four times as tall as the average pony. “We can check the card catalog!”

Dash drifted back a bit to take it in. “I’m glad you’re comfortable with your wings, Twilight, but how’s somepony without ‘em supposed to use this?”

“Oh, there’s a rolling ladder on the other side. I just tucked it back there for aesthetics.” Twilight took flight, flapping up to “Characters, C-D,” and pulled out the drawer. It extended out a good dozen ponylengths.

Dash frowned as she came up to Twilight’s right side. “Yeaaaaah, not to sound like AJ, but this doesn’t seem all that practical.”

“Oh, it just takes some getting used to. Every revolutionary idea does. Now, Daring Do…” Twilight moved to the middle of the drawer and soon found the tabbed index card she was looking for. “There we are.” She glanced at Rainbow and grinned. “And thanks to the entangled quantification divination, the catalogue’s always up to date!”

“Uh huh,” came the flat response.

Twilight rolled her eyes. Some ponies just couldn’t appreciate the little things. “Looks we’ve got… about eleven hundred or so books involving Daring.”

All the enthusiasm that had drained out of Rainbow came rushing back. “Awesome! That should tide me over for a weekend. Where are they?”

“That’s a more complicated question than you’d think,” said Twilight. She moved back to the center of library. “It’s all about how you approach them.”

“I mean, I’m gonna read them,” Rainbow said as she trailed after her. “Not like I’m gonna use ‘em to make papier-mache. You’d pluck me bald if I tried.”

Twilight nodded. She could certainly recognize *that* attempt to get her goat. “And I know you do it to yourself if you hurt a friend like that. But I mean it literally. The hyperbolic geometry and subjective gravity in here isn’t just for show. If you reorient yourself like so…” Twilight took Rainbow’s hooves in hers and started adjusting her pitch, yaw, and roll. Also several other reorientations that she didn’t have words for. Yet.

Rainbow swallowed and shook her head. “Okay, *that’s* gonna take some getting used too…”

“And now the shelves are sorted by character!” Twilight flew off into the subtly different stacks, Rainbow following close behind. Once they arrived at rows of khaki-colored covers, she smiled and said “Feel free to ask how to find the books about *you.*”

“Nah,” Rainbow said, already browsing the shelf.

Twilight blinked in surprise. “Really? I thought you’d love to see those.”

“Why should I read about me doing cool stuff when I can just go out and do cool stuff? Nah, I’d rather read about Daring without bothering her while she works on the next book in the main series.” That decided, Dash pulled out one of the books, flipped open to the middle of it, took one look, and blushed like a tomato. She slammed it shut and shoved it back on the shelf. “Um. I, uh, I think the place needs a few more adjustments.”

Twilight cleared her throat. She had planned out that lecture for a *reason*, darn it. “Right. So. About the rating system…”
