# The Tomboy and the Pretty Mare

Applejack said, “Tell me this ain’t happening. It’s happening. Hee hee! Oh, this is gonna be good!” She raised her voice and called out, “Everypony! Gather round! I got a good feeling about this one!”

“What is it?” asked Rarity.

Applejack smirked as five ponies crowded around her, trying to look at the manuscript. “Just you sit back and listen.”

> The Tomboy and the Pretty Mare  
> By Granny Smith

Rainbow Dash said, “Granny Smith wrote about us? What do you think she wrote?”

Applejack said, “I don’t know, but I’m itching to find out!”

> That morning, Rainbow Dash actually done pull a brush through her mane once or twice. She didn’t normally bother. Being a tomboy has its advantages, and one of them is, if another mare has a mind to give you a dirty look, you can just buck ‘em in the shins. So most days Rainbow Dash didn’t brush her mane. Actually, she looked better when she was a little mussed-up like that. It made her look like she had been working out (which she had been, of course) and reminded you how athletic she was. You was like to see sweat glistening on her flanks. What flanks! She had a sleek, toned body that most mares would die for. When she flew by, everypony would stop what they was doing and watch. Her mane whipped in the wind, and her wings pumped up and down, and it wasn’t a single pony saw her who didn’t think to themselves, “Now this here’s a real mare.” Why, everypony could’ve just stood there for hours watching that body of hers.

Rainbow Dash said, “That’s not exactly what I expected. Yeah. Uh. Why is Granny Smith talking about my body like that?”

Applejack snorted and said, “It’s a story. She ain’t talking about your body, she’s narrating. There’s a difference.”

> But today, brushing her mane was important. She was fixing to meet Rarity, after all. She told herself it was just lunch and that her excitement was perfectly normal for somepony fixing to go to lunch with a friend. It wasn’t nothing strange at all. But she wouldn’t have brushed her mane for nopony else.
> 
> Rarity made other mares jealous, but it wasn’t her fault. Lots of mares wanted to look pretty, but Rarity was prettier than all of them. Rarity would’ve been pretty even if she didn’t try. And she was so sweet that she didn’t never lord her looks over the other mares. She was a living, breathing work of art. It wasn’t never a single hair out of place on her, not in her coat and not in her mane. The way her tail done curl, it made a little bounce when she walked. That little bounce always drew ponies’ attention, so everypony ended up looking at her hindquarters. It wasn’t no shame in looking at Rarity’s hindquarters because nopony could stop themselves. They were just too pretty. And when everypony’s doing it, stallion and mare alike, then it’s the most normal thing in Equestria. It don’t matter whether you reckon a mare ought to stare at Rarity’s hindquarters. Fact is, that’s what all mares do, and that makes it as normal as applesauce.

Rarity was blushing, and not from her makeup. “I don’t think I’ve ever noticed… that,” she said.

Applejack said, “It’s just a story! It don’t mean nothing! Anyway.”

> For months, Rainbow Dash had longed to tell Rarity how she felt. Rainbow Dash wasn’t no shy mare, not normally, but every time she was around Rarity, she got too gosh-darned nervous to say anything. The very thought of Rarity made Rainbow Dash feel all funny inside. Her heart started a-pounding, and her hooves started a-quaking, and her wings started a-stiffening. Why, the thought of Rarity could nearly knock Rainbow Dash out of the air!

Rainbow Dash asked, “You sure this doesn’t mean anything?”

“I said it’s just a story! Granny Smith got the right to write a story, same as anypony else!”

> Meanwhile, Rarity was having unladylike thoughts about Rainbow Dash again. Every night, and most days, too, she had thoughts that were downright scandalous. She dreamed about watching Rainbow Dash’s workouts. When Rainbow Dash was done, she’d set down next to Rarity, exhausted and breathing hard. Rarity’d feel the heat radiating off of Rainbow Dash’s tired muscles. She’d nuzzle Rainbow Dash, putting her muzzle up right close to her friend’s, and Rainbow Dash would relax and lean into her. Then Rarity’d run her hoof through Rainbow Dash’s unkempt mane and stroke her wings and say, “You look tired. Would a massage help?” They’d go somewhere private, where nopony was gonna disturb them, and Rarity would gently rub down her friend’s body, rub her nice and gentle all over, working out all the knots. Rainbow Dash’d give the cutest little moan of pleasure, because she liked what Rarity was doing, liked it very much, you see. She’d stretch her legs, and just by accident their hooves’d touch. Then—

Applejack set the manuscript down. “I don’t know how I feel about this story.”

Fluttershy said, “Um, I think Granny Smith can write a story like that if she wants. And since she went to that trouble, we should try to read it.”

Applejack said, “I don’t know. This ain’t what I was reckoned it was gonna be.”

Rarity stammered, “You know—er—well—it doesn’t do any harm to keep reading. I’m—not offended or anything like that. Are you, Rainbow Dash?”

“No,” Rainbow Dash said. “I don’t think I’m offended. I mean, there’s nothing to be offended by. It’s just a story. Just a story. I’ve never had thoughts like that myself. Not even once. Not me.”

“Me either,” said Rarity.

Applejack said, “Maybe I’ll just skip over this part. Let’s pick up where there’s some action.”

> Applejack said, “You two are cuter together than a whole litter of puppies. I been hoping y’all would get together for months now. I don’t just reckon you ought to be a couple, I know it. When two mares are as much in love as y’all are, you got to.”
> 
> You see, Applejack’s family had taken care to raise her right. They was all of them Apples deep in their hearts, even her Ma (despite being raised one of them doggone Pears), so they was all good, honest folks. From the time she was a foal, they had taught her how a pony ought to behave, and Applejack had grown up to be a good, honest mare just like them. Over and over, she proved that she—

“Sorry, Granny,” muttered Applejack, “but I’m gonna skip that.”

> When Applejack saw them mares in love, she did what any Apple would’ve done. Every fiber in her being told her that—

> Because Applejack had real Apple family values. Some folks says they gots family values, but they gets ornery when you—

> Applejack continued, “What’s at the core of being an Apple is—”

> “So that ain’t no reason not to date her neither. Next, consider that—”

Applejack lowered the manuscript and sighed. “I’m just gonna skip all this.” She flipped through a few pages before resuming.

> Rainbow Dash’s lips kissed their way around Rarity’s neck. They followed the shape of her crest, crossed over her withers, and came back around to her forehead. When Rainbow Dash’s lips brushed over Rarity’s horn, Rarity done shivered herself in anticipation.
> 
> Rainbow Dash whispered straight in Rarity’s ear. “Ready?”
> 
> Rarity’s voice stuck in her throat, but she nodded. She hadn’t never imagined this here day might come. Rainbow Dash lowered her muzzle. Rarity made a right powerful squeal of pleasure as their lips touched—

“What in horse apples is this?” yelled Applejack.

Fluttershy said, “Um, I think you should keep going.”

“Me too,” said Rainbow Dash.

“Me three,” said Rarity.

Applejack mumbled, “Bless your heart, Granny.” She sighed as she adjusted her hat. “Alright. If y’all really want me to. But I’m gonna skip over this part.” Applejack read silently for a few moments. Suddenly she gasped and her cheeks turned flaming red. “I ain’t reading this! This ain’t no story! This is just—just—just filth!” Applejack flung the manuscript into the wastebasket. “I don’t know what Granny Smith was thinking. I’m mighty sorry about this, y’all.”

“We’re not bothered,” said Rarity. “I mean, I’m not bothered. I can’t speak for Dashie—for Rainbow Dash, I mean—”

“I’m not bothered,” interrupted Rainbow Dash. “It’s just a story. Rares and I—I mean, Rarity and I have never had intimate moments like that. Ever. Not even in our dreams. And we’ve never tried to keep anything like that secret from everypony else. Not us. We’d never do anything like that.” Rarity lightly kicked Rainbow Dash’s hoof. “I mean, uh, yeah. That is. Heh. Er…” Another kick. “Never mind.”

Fluttershy, who had years of experience staying unnoticed and in the background, waited until the other ponies had returned to looking at manuscripts. After a few minutes, they were reading, sorting, shuffling, everything except looking at her. Soundlessly, she crept up to the wastebasket where Applejack had tossed the story. She grabbed it in her mouth and, with a silent flick of her head, hid it under her wing. Then she left, quietly and without being noticed, looking for somewhere private.

The first moment Applejack happened to be alone in the library, she went rooting through the wastebasket looking for Granny Smith's manuscript. "Horse feathers," she muttered. "Maybe Granny's got another copy."
