# My Hero Applejack

Rainbow Dash idly sifted through the latest box of books, when she came across something unusual: an Equestrian Mail Service flat-rate envelope, addressed to the Castle, care of Applejack. That wasn’t particularly strange, except in this case, the postage had been skillfully drawn on. *In crayon*. And the return address was… Derpy’s place? “Hey AJ, I think this one’s for you?” she called out.

The big farmpony ambled over, scratching her mane. “Huh. Well, give ‘er here.” She tore open the cardboard envelope, revealing a foal’s craft project; sheets of construction paper, threaded together with twine, into a loose pamphlet-like book. And proudly embossed on the cover in gold glitter-glue, read *My Hero Applejack*.

“Well, gonna read it?” Rainbow Dash asked, struggling to peer over the larger mare’s shoulder.

“Yeah, of course.” Applejack flipped the booklet open, careful not to damage it. The inside revealed a hoof-crafted picture book, words and images drawn in more crayon, with a simple, heartfelt dedication.

“To My Mother.”

***

Many moons ago, when I was a littler filly than I am now, my mom made a mistake. She was working with Ms. Rainbow Dash to clear the skies over Town Hall, when she accidentally zapped it!

> Beneath the text was a drawing of Ponyville’s own Derpy Hooves, happily bouncing atop a dense gray storm cloud; jagged yellow lightning bolts rained down on Ponyville’s terraced town hall, which was rendered in as exacting detail as macaroni could muster.

Mom’s bosses were very upset. Some even yelled at her! They said if she didn’t find a way to fix Town Hall, she’d be fired. Or fined. Or both!

> A circle of angry ponies’ faces surrounded a nervous-looking Derpy. Central to them was a ghastly rendition of Mayor Mare, flames in her eyes as she shot some sort of deathray at the poor pegasus!

Mom was very sad. Sadder than even a hug and kiss and pat could fix. She was so sad, she drank some of the funny-tasting cider I’m not supposed to have. She took a nap, and didn’t wake up until the next morning. That’s when she told me we might have to move to a smaller house again.

> The page conspicuously lacked a picture, but the paper itself was marred with a few small circles of water damage.

But all was not lost! The very next day, Ms. Applejack promised the whole town she would win every prize ever at the rodeo, and use the money to fix Town Hall. She set off to Canterlot… but never came back!

> The medium changed to colored pencils; ponies readily identifiable as Twilight, Rarity, Rainbow Dash, Pinkie, and Fluttershy all gawped at an empty train car, its doors open wide at the Ponyville platform.

Because it turned out, Applejack didn’t win anything! She made a promise she couldn’t keep. She didn’t know what went wrong. But! She didn’t give up! Applejack ~~jepo~~ ~~jeoap~~ risked her friendship with her friends to keep her promise. She worked on a cherry farm, day and night, to raise enough money to return home.

> Applejack, in profile, galloped at full tilt within a pony-sized treadmill, driving the cherry sorting machines. Behind her, pinned to the wall, were a dozen medals; second place, fourth place, participant, but no blue ribbons.

It may have taken her a bit longer than she’d planned, but she kept her promise. Applejack had already helped save Ponyville a couple times, and she’d go on to help save Ponyville a few times more. But on that day, she was my hero.

***

“’...and mom still doesn’t know what went wrong,’” Applejack read aloud.

The last page of the book was a silly drawing of Derpy and Dinky throwing Applejack into the air, shouting, “hip hip hurray!”

The farmpony covered her mouth with her hooves, her eyes taking on the slightest hint of mistiness.

“I know that look,” Rainbow Dash teased, “That’s your ‘crying on the inside’ look.”

“An’ what if Ah am?” Applejack shot back. She took a breath, then softened her tone, “Ah know Ah made a heckuva lotta ponies’ days with th’ cash Ah brought back, but Ah guess Ah hadn’t considered how much of a bind Derpy was in.”

Dash’s ears drooped. “Yeah, that was a pretty dark time for her. I was mad at her, the *whole town* was mad. And to think, this was before Tirek wrecked the Golden Oak. Before we thought the town would ever really come under attack.”

“Yeah… y’know, sometimes I miss those day- oh wait! Hang on, remember the one-two kick where Twilight’s tried ta ‘fix’ the Parasprites, an’ they started eatin’ the town, an’ then Spike got all *big* on us an’ wrecked the town all over again?”

“Ah yeah, that was, like, a month later. Dang, now I’m not sure what I was mad about!”

A comfortable silence fell over the two mares.

“So, onto the next one?” Rainbow Dash eventually asked.

Applejack eyed the stacks upon stacks of unread stories. “...can ya give me a minute? Ah think there’s somepony Ah wanna talk to.”

“Heh, sure. Oh hey, maybe she’ll buy you a cider!”

“That doesn’t sound half bad. Arright, see ya in a bit.” When the farmer was halfway out the door, she called back, “An’ if there *is* cider involved, Ah’ll be sure to bring you back a mug.”

“And that’s why you’re the best, AJ.”
