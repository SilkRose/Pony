# (Untitled)

Rainbow Dash stared at the page before her, trying to comprehend it, constantly failing. Its meaning was beyond her. She spent a minute going back and forth over merely the first line, attempting to untangle the words. She came away with nothing. Whatever ideas were behind that ink, she couldn’t find them.

Not because it was dense prose, mind. She literally couldn’t read it. The penmareship was horrible.

That there was an “e”. She was pretty sure that was an “e”. Or maybe it was an “l”. Mmmmaybe. She tilted her head. Maybe an “o”? Or a “G”. Hmm.

…It was a letter, right?

Rainbow’s eyes flitted over the rest of the page. More illegibility. Pen strokes blurred into one another, always just barely not a recognizable letter. They were separated into chunks that seemed to be words but never were. Possibly. They’d be words if only she could read them, surely. Surely.

She fanned through the pages. To call what she saw a scrawl would be to insult scrawl. Lines wiggled and wavered across the paper in intricate patterns that brushed up against legibility. She couldn’t make head or tail of it. Still she kept “reading” it. She wasn’t going to let bad mouthwriting get the better of her. No way. Nuh-uh.

Nope.

Definitely not. Totally.

…

Pretty lines.

At some point, Rainbow had battered her head so hard against the idea that something in her mind cracked and the possibility that this was actually good began creeping in. One of those heady Literary Art Pieces with meaning dripping from every little swoop and curl. With all the swoops and curls here, this wouldn’t just be smart; it would be a work of sheer genius utterly beyond her comprehension.

Because the obtuseness would be the point. Something about the equine condition. What was the term for weird for the sake of weird? Oh, yeah: postmodernism. (Probably.) This was totally postmodern. Had to be. As awesome as she was, this was just too smart for her. Someone with an actual head for analysis would have a field day with it.

Eventually, finally, Rainbow swallowed her pride and conceded. This was too smart for her. She turned to the pony next to her. “Hey, Twilight, could you explain this to me?”

***

Thirty seconds later…

“I’ve read more legible chicken scratch from actual chickens!”

Thank Celestia. It was just a load of roadapples.

“More legible doctors’ notes!” Twilight turned the page upside down. From the way her expression didn’t change, that didn’t help at all.

“So that’s a ‘no’,” said Rainbow.

Twilight tilted the page again. She was running out of orientations. “I… can’t even be sure this is Ponish. Or even the Romane alphabet! It’s definitely not Zebran… I’m pretty sure it’s not Old Horse… Who wrote this?” She shut the book to take a look at the cover.

For a rather long time.

“I can’t read this,” she squeaked, her voice almost too high to hear. “I can’t read this. I can’t read ANY of this…”

“Uh…” Rainbow waved a hoof in front of Twilight’s face. Twilight didn’t uncurl or stop rocking back and forth or even blink. “You okay, Twi?”

“I can’t sort this. I don’t know the contents. The Hoofy Decimal System is useless.”

“You’re not okay. Uh, gir-”

Pinkie was already over before Rainbow could finish her thought. “It goes under ‘O’,” Pinkie declared sagely.

Twilight twitched out of her fugue. “Why O?”

“Because someday,” Pinkie said, sweeping her hoof theatrically, “some pony is going to come walking in, and they’ll ask if you got their book. You’ll ask what it was about, and they’ll give this long answer, like super long you wouldn’t even believe, like if a sentence is a journey, this is one of those that takes the scenic route at every opportunity and never seems to actually be going anywhere and they just keep going on and on and on-”

Rainbow’s cough sounded oddly like, Get on with it!

“They’ll give that answer,” said Pinkie, “and because this book’s just as hard to get, you’ll immediately go, ‘Oh, yeah, that one!’ because that’s the only way you know how to think about it. So you can go to ‘O’. Or you’ll be asked what the worst book in the library is, and you’ll go, ‘Oh, yeah, that one!’ So: ‘O’.”

“There’s a lot of books left to cover, Pinkie. How do you know this one’s the worst already?”

“Twilight,” said Pinkie seriously. “This is a book you can’t. Read.”

“…We’ll need to do some categorizing to be sure it’s the worst worst,” said Twilight. “But…” She looked at the book again, then carefully set it down as far away from the others as she could manage. “O. It’s under O.”

Rainbow chuckled halfheartedly. “Sorry about that, Twi,” she muttered, rubbing the back of her neck.

But Twilight just shrugged. “Eh. Somepony had to get the worst one.” (“Told you!” said Pinkie.) She picked through the piles for another book. “At least it’s only getting better from here, right?”
